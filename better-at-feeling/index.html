A warm-up for the lab talk

Feeling better, or better at feeling?

Telling someone to "just accept your feelings" is everywhere: in therapy, in apps, in advice between friends. On December 1 I'll ask what accepting a feeling actually does. Here is a five-minute way in, whatever your background.

Sagit Zilberman, Tamir LabLab talk · December 1, 2026

Play with the water first

The hand is fighting the waves.
The stoneSomething happens: a deadline, a criticism, a sad memory.
The wavesThe emotion it stirs up.
The handHow you react to that emotion. Pushing back at it, or letting it be.

In "fight" mode you can also drag across the water to push it yourself. What keeps the water moving? And when the hand rests, what changes, and what doesn't?

A 60-second experiment on yourself

Think of something mildly annoying from this week. Something small: a slow reply, a bus that didn't come. Let the feeling come up a little. Then try each of these for about 20 seconds.

A. Make it go away

Talk yourself out of it, think of something else, push it down.

B. Let it be there

Notice where you feel it. Don't judge it, and don't try to change it.

Then ask yourself: after B, was the feeling weaker? Or was it the same feeling, held differently? Keep your answer in mind.

The puzzle

A paradox at the heart of acceptance

Emotion regulation

"The defining feature of emotion regulation is the activation of a goal to influence the emotion trajectory."

Gross, 2015
vs.
Emotional acceptance

Engaging with one's emotions without judging them and without trying to influence them.

Bishop et al., 2004; Hayes et al., 2006; Lindsay & Creswell, 2017

Acceptance is often called an emotion regulation strategy. But if regulation means trying to change emotions, and acceptance means not trying, how can acceptance be regulation?

Something to chew on meanwhile

Questions to think about before the talk

If you accept a feeling so that it will go away, are you still accepting it?

Is letting a feeling be something you do, or something you stop doing?

What would count as acceptance "working"? Feeling less, or something else?

Does accepting an emotion mean accepting the situation that caused it?

If someone keeps their hand in ice water longer, does it hurt less, or does the hurt matter less?

Should a therapist promise that accepting will bring relief?

Words you'll hear

Open the mini-glossary
Emotion regulation
Attempts to influence which emotions we have, when we have them, and how we experience or express them. Its defining feature is a goal to influence the emotion (Gross, 2015).
Process model
Gross's framework that sorts regulation strategies into five families, by the point in the emotion-generative process where they have their main impact: situation selection, situation modification, attentional deployment, cognitive change, and response modulation (Gross, 2015).
Reappraisal
A form of cognitive change: changing the meaning of an emotion-eliciting situation in order to change its emotional impact (Gross, 1998), for example "it's practice, not a test."
Suppression
Expressive suppression, a form of response modulation, refers to ongoing efforts to inhibit one's emotion-expressive behavior (Gross, 2015). Some experiments instead ask people to suppress the feeling itself (experiential suppression).
Rumination
Repetitively and passively focusing on one's distress and on its possible causes and consequences (Nolen-Hoeksema et al., 2008). In experiments, it tends to intensify unpleasant emotions.
Meta-analysis
A statistical way of combining many studies into one estimate, weighting the more precise ones more.
Effect size (g)
Hedges' g: the difference between two groups in standard-deviation units, corrected for small samples. By convention, about 0.2 is small, 0.5 medium, and 0.8 large (Cohen, 1988).
Preregistration
Registering hypotheses and analysis plans publicly before analyzing the data, so the results can't reshape the questions.
